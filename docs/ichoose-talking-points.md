# #IChoose 365 — Talking and Interview Points

Use these five stories on every podcast, panel, workshop, free talk and long-form post. Each one is tied to a #IChoose statement from the journal, so your daily content and your speaking content reinforce each other.

---

## 1. "#IChoose to honour my own pace"

**The story: the discharge cliff**

"The day my rehab appointments stopped, I looked fine on paper. The functional scores were good. But inside, I was standing at the edge of a cliff. The professionals had gone, the plan was on a folded sheet of paper, and nobody was checking whether I could actually keep it going. That is the moment MyRhythm is built for — not the appointment itself, but the quiet, lonely gap after it ends."

**The teaching point:**
The gap between "clinical-ready" and "life-ready" is where people lose the plan. Productivity tools assume an ideal brain; MyRhythm assumes a real one.

**The close:**
"I wrote that experience into the #IChoose 365 journal — one small choice a day — and I'm building MyRhythm so nobody has to cross that bridge alone."

---

## 2. "#IChoose one clear next step"

**The story: why plans on paper fail**

"I used to come away from appointments with a list. 'Drink more water, walk every day, call the GP, sort the medication.' Noble list. And completely useless. Nothing was dated, nothing was owned, nothing reminded me. By day three the paper was in a drawer and I was pretending I'd forgotten on purpose. MyRhythm turns a conversation into one owned, dated next step at a time — because one step with a date beats ten good intentions."

**The teaching point:**
Good intentions are not a plan. A plan needs an owner, a date, and a reminder. MyRhythm extracts that from the conversation itself.

**The close:**
"That is what the #IChoose journal trains you to do — choose one clear next step — and it is what MyRhythm automates after the conversation."

---

## 3. "#IChoose to receive support"

**The story: the family member becoming the memory for two people**

"There is a person in almost every recovery story who is never on the discharge form: the partner, the daughter, the friend who becomes the memory for two people. They hold the appointments, the medications, the questions, the emotions. And they burn out quietly because nobody asked how they were doing. MyRhythm's Support Circle is designed for them — not to dump everything on them, but to give them exactly the right visibility, with permission, and a way to leave encouragement without becoming the full-time secretary."

**The teaching point:**
No one walks alone. Support is not a weakness; it is a design choice. The right support is visible, bounded, and consensual.

**The close:**
"The #IChoose journal has a whole thread on choosing support — and MyRhythm makes that support safe, visible, and calm."

---

## 4. "#IChoose progress over perfection"

**The story: the ideal-brain assumption in every productivity tool**

"Every productivity app I tried assumed I woke up with a clear head, a full battery, and eight uninterrupted hours. I did not. Some days my brain felt sharp at 9am; some days it did not show up until 2pm. The apps punished me for that. MyRhythm asks how your brain is today and schedules the demanding stuff for when you are actually sharp — while still letting you override it when your client can only meet at 4pm. Progress, not perfection."

**The teaching point:**
Most tools are built for an ideal brain. MyRhythm is built for a variable brain — and that turns out to be useful for everyone, not just people recovering from a health event.

**The close:**
"That belief is in every page of the #IChoose journal — and it is the engine inside MyRhythm's rhythm-aware scheduling."

---

## 5. "#IChoose to be my own steady person"

**The story: identity after a health event**

"After a health event, the world wants to measure you. Functional scores, discharge notes, return-to-work dates. But nobody measures the thing that actually hurts: the loss of trust in your own mind. Who am I if I cannot rely on my memory? MyRhythm is not a medical device. It does not fix your brain. It gives you back a steady, reliable place to keep what matters — so you can stop fearing that you will let yourself down."

**The teaching point:**
The real loss is often identity, not function. MyRhythm restores confidence by making the plan external, traceable, and owned by the person.

**The close:**
"The #IChoose journal starts with the daily choice to be your own steady person. MyRhythm is the digital companion that makes that choice easier to keep."

## How to use these in any talk

**Short form (60 seconds):**
Pick one story. State the #IChoose line. Tell the moment. Give the teaching point. Close with the journal or the founding list.

**Long form (20-minute workshop):**
Open with story 1. Use story 2 to show the method. Use story 3 to bring in support. Use story 4 to handle the "but I'm not injured" objection. Use story 5 to land the emotional core.

**Podcast interview:**
Let the host's question choose the story, but always bridge back to the same close: the journal and the founding list.

**The close for every appearance:**
"If this resonates, two things you can do: get the #IChoose 365 journal — it is the daily practice — and join the MyRhythm founding list, because the app that keeps your plan going after the appointment ends is almost ready."

## Objection handles

**"Isn't this just a notes app?"**
"A notes app gives you text. MyRhythm gives you owned, dated, reminded next steps that trace back to the conversation they came from. The journal is the daily mindset; the app is the follow-through machine."

**"Is it a medical device?"**
"No. It does not diagnose, treat or fix anything. It is a productivity and follow-through companion for the messy bit after the appointment."

**"Will it overwhelm someone with memory problems?"**
"Three choices maximum per screen, one primary action, large touch targets, first-person language. It is built for the person who feels overwhelmed by everything else."

**"I'm not recovering from anything — is this for me?"**
"Yes. If you have ever walked out of a meeting and forgotten what you agreed to, you have felt the same gap. MyRhythm was built from a brain-health perspective, but the problem — losing what was said — is universal."
