# Critical market comparison: MyRhythm vs Granola, Plaud and adjacent apps

Produce an evidence-led commercial assessment of whether MyRhythm is genuinely different enough to win attention, rather than simply listing its features.

## Comparison set

- **Granola** — meeting capture, enhanced notes, actions, searchable meeting memory and team collaboration.
- **Plaud** — hardware-led, always-ready voice capture, transcription, summaries and large template library.
- **Otter** — established meeting transcription, searchable meeting knowledge and calendar-connected meeting capture.
- **Tiimo** — the closest adjacent benchmark for accessible, neuroinclusive planning and follow-through.
- Use generic calendars, task managers and clinical cognitive-training apps only where they expose an important boundary in MyRhythm's position.

## Assessment

1. Compare each product across the customer journey: capture, understanding, action extraction, scheduling, reminders, adaptation to cognitive capacity, shared support, traceability and continuity after an appointment.
2. Separate **features competitors already match** from **combinations they do not visibly offer**. Do not claim that an individual MyRhythm feature is unique when it is not.
3. Test MyRhythm's proposed differentiation:
   - Memory Bridge: conversation → editable next steps → dates → calendar → reminders.
   - Brain-health-informed, overridable scheduling.
   - Support Circle permissions and encouragement.
   - Source-to-action traceability and the dated diary.
   - Pre/post-discharge continuity and user-controlled retention.
   - Memory-First Design™ for people with variable cognitive capacity, while remaining relevant to anyone who loses actions after conversations.
4. Score MyRhythm honestly on product clarity, functional depth, trust, ease, proof, distribution and defensibility.
5. Identify where Granola, Plaud, Otter or Tiimo are presently stronger, especially recording reliability, polish, integrations, trust evidence, installed audience and simplicity.
6. Give a direct verdict: whether MyRhythm currently stands out as a product, merely as a concept, or not yet—and what evidence would change that verdict.

## Commercial recommendation

- Define the narrow first customer and buying moment MyRhythm should own.
- Recommend one external promise led by Memory Bridge and life follow-through; keep the 4C loop as the internal experience rather than the opening pitch.
- State the three or fewer capabilities that should dominate the founding test, and which broader features should remain secondary.
- Name the proof needed before making stronger claims: successful real-device captures, action accuracy, schedule follow-through, user comprehension, Support Circle permission safety and repeat use.
- Provide a concise comparison table, an uncompromising verdict, the strongest defensible opening, and the five most important next moves.

## Evidence and limits

Use current official product pages and UK Semrush estimates captured on 11 September 2026. Treat search figures as directional estimates, not market demand forecasts. Distinguish observed competitor capabilities, confirmed MyRhythm build status, and strategic interpretation. Preserve the non-medical-claims rule throughout.
