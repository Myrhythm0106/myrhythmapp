# MyRhythm — Competitive Differentiation Assessment

**Assessment date:** 11 September 2026  
**Comparison set:** Granola, Plaud, Otter and Tiimo  
**Purpose:** A critical founder-level assessment of whether MyRhythm is sufficiently different to earn attention and adoption.

## Executive verdict

**Yes, MyRhythm has enough conceptual differentiation to stand out — but it has not yet proved enough operational differentiation to claim a defensible market position.**

MyRhythm is not differentiated by recording, transcription, summaries, action extraction, calendars, reminders, AI planning or accessible design on their own. Stronger, better-funded products already perform each of those jobs.

Its credible opening is the **combination**:

> An important conversation becomes an editable, owned plan; each next step remains traceable to its source; timing adapts to the person's present capacity but can always be overridden; reminders and selected supporters help the plan continue after the appointment ends.

That is meaningfully different from a meeting note-taker, recorder or planner. It joins activities that are usually fragmented across several products and applies Memory-First Design™ to the point at which people commonly lose continuity.

However, this is presently a **promising wedge, not a proven moat**. Granola and Plaud can credibly claim superior capture polish, Plaud has dedicated hardware, Otter has mature meeting workflows, and Tiimo has stronger recognition in neuroinclusive planning. MyRhythm earns its position only when the entire conversation-to-follow-through chain works reliably and simply on real phones.

## What is not unique

MyRhythm should never lead with these as if competitors do not have them:

1. Recording a conversation.
2. Transcribing or summarising it.
3. Extracting action items, owners and deadlines.
4. Searching previous conversations.
5. Connecting a calendar.
6. Sending reminders.
7. Using AI to help create a plan.
8. Designing for neurodivergent or cognitively overloaded users.

Calling these unique would weaken trust. They are necessary product ingredients, not the position.

## What could be defensibly different

### 1. The complete continuity chain

MyRhythm connects **Capture → Commit → Calibrate → Celebrate** around one personally meaningful thread. The action is not merely extracted; it is reviewed, owned, dated, scheduled, reminded, completed and retained with its source.

The 4C loop should remain the internal product architecture. It is too abstract to be the first external message.

### 2. Memory Bridge as the visible proof

Memory Bridge is the clearest demonstration of the difference:

```text
Important conversation
        ↓
Editable write-up and agreed next steps
        ↓
Owners, dates, calendar and reminders
        ↓
Optional Support Circle involvement
        ↓
Completion and a traceable personal record
```

Competitors cover parts of this chain. MyRhythm can stand out by making the whole chain feel like one calm experience.

### 3. Capacity-aware but human-controlled scheduling

The Brain Health self-check can make scheduling suggestions fit the person's current rhythm and capacity. This is useful only if it remains advisory: a client, employer or family member may be available at an inconvenient time, and the user must be able to choose it without warnings, penalties or repeated prompts.

### 4. Personal continuity with permission boundaries

The Support Circle is not a team workspace. Its role is to help the individual retain ownership while giving selected people access only to what the individual chooses. That can matter greatly after an appointment, during a return to work, or when a busy person needs another human to help a commitment survive.

### 5. Universal relevance from a specific foundation

The primary design standard remains people experiencing memory or cognitive challenges. The broader promise is nevertheless familiar: **important conversations create actions that disappear once ordinary life resumes**. That applies after rehabilitation, but also after a client meeting, school meeting, care appointment, return-to-work discussion or demanding week.

## The customer and moment to own first

### Primary founding customer

An adult leaving neuro rehabilitation, or already home after a neurological event, who can participate in plans but has difficulty retaining, organising and following through on what was agreed. A trusted family member or supporter may be involved with permission.

### The buying or adoption moment

The moment immediately before discharge, at a follow-up appointment, or when the person and family realise that paper notes and ordinary reminders are not enough to keep the plan alive at home.

This is narrower than “anyone who needs help planning.” That focus is an advantage. Broader audiences can recognise themselves later without weakening the founding proof.

### The broader bridge

After the first use case is proven, the same chain can credibly serve:

- a professional with ADHD whose meeting actions disappear;
- a carer managing repeated appointments;
- an employee returning after a health event;
- a student managing support-plan commitments;
- a busy professional whose important conversations fail to become completed actions.

These are expansion hypotheses until tested. They should not displace the founding use case.

## Recommended external position

### One promise

> **MyRhythm keeps your plan going after the appointment ends.**

### Supporting explanation

> Capture the conversation, review what matters, and turn agreed next steps into a plan you can actually follow—with timing that respects your day and support from people you choose.

### What should not lead

- “AI meeting notes” — makes MyRhythm look like a smaller Granola or Otter.
- “Voice recorder” — invites direct comparison with Plaud's hardware and capture quality.
- “Visual planner” — moves the comparison onto Tiimo's strongest ground.
- The 4C terminology — valuable inside the app, but too abstract as the first explanation.
- Brain-health scores — supporting personalisation, not the principal reason to buy.
- “Collaborative Cognitive Continuity layer” — useful internal category architecture, not the opening consumer line.

## The founding test must prove only three things

1. **I do not lose the conversation.** Recording and recovery work reliably on real phones, including interrupted and long captures.
2. **I leave with the right next steps.** The write-up and extracted actions are accurate enough to review quickly, edit easily and trust.
3. **The plan continues without taking over my life.** Dates, real-calendar awareness, reminders and selected Support Circle involvement help actions happen without creating overwhelm.

Diary, assessment trends, reports and retention controls support trust in those three outcomes. They should not become competing journeys during the founding test.

## Five moves that matter now

### 1. Freeze the founding experience

Do not add another visible feature before testing. The product should guide a new user through assessment → first capture → review → schedule → reminder → completion, with the diary as reassurance that nothing is lost.

### 2. Prove capture under real conditions

Test iPhone and Android sessions of 15 minutes, 60 minutes and the promised maximum, including screen lock, an incoming call, low connectivity, an accidental close and recovery. Record loss must be treated as a launch-stopping fault.

### 3. Measure action quality and comprehension

For each founding capture, ask whether the important commitments, owners and dates were correctly identified; how much editing was needed; and whether the person knew what to do next without help.

### 4. Prove the follow-through loop

Measure whether scheduled actions reach the real calendar, reminders arrive, users complete or consciously move them, and users return to the source when context is needed. This is the evidence that separates MyRhythm from a note-taking demo.

### 5. Establish trust before institutional selling

Complete the two-account Support Circle permission test, Google/Outlook production configuration, independent accessibility review and independent security review. Rehabilitation organisations will compare MyRhythm's trust posture with established vendors, not with an early prototype.

## What evidence would justify a stronger claim

A credible first proof set would include:

- at least 20 founding users completing the full loop;
- at least 100 important conversations captured across real devices;
- no unrecovered recording loss;
- a clear majority confirming that extracted next steps required only minor edits;
- evidence that scheduled next steps were completed, moved intentionally or shared—not simply forgotten;
- repeat use over four weeks rather than one successful demonstration;
- zero unauthorised Support Circle access in two-account permission testing;
- qualitative evidence that users felt more in control and less dependent on remembering everything themselves.

These are proposed validation thresholds, not promises of clinical or life outcomes.

## Final answer

**MyRhythm is different enough to deserve a focused founding test. It is not yet different enough in proven delivery to assume the market will notice or trust it.**

Granola, Plaud, Otter and Tiimo each own a clear, easy-to-repeat idea. MyRhythm can own an equally clear territory: **life follow-through after an important conversation**. The defensible product is not the recorder, assessment, calendar or Support Circle separately. It is the calm, reliable continuity between them.

If MyRhythm keeps adding breadth, it risks becoming an impressive but confusing collection of features. If it freezes the scope and proves that one continuity chain for people who cannot afford to lose the thread, it has a credible and valuable place in the market.
